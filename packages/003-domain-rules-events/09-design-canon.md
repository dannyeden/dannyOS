# HealthOS Design Canon

**Status:** Proposed design principles

## Promise

HealthOS should feel calm, trustworthy, beautiful, and optimistic—like walking
into the Garden of Eden. Beauty is not decoration layered over complexity; it is
the clear expression of safe choices, longitudinal progress, and human support.

## Principles

### Calm over busy

Show the most important present state and next action first. Progressive disclosure
holds detail without hiding risk, uncertainty, cost, or clinical responsibility.

### Guidance over complexity

Translate the system's structure into understandable steps. Never remove meaningful
choice or disguise a decision merely to simplify a screen.

### Longitudinal over transactional

Orient experiences around health progress, continuity, and the Timeline. Purchases,
messages, labs, and shipments are moments within a relationship, not the whole story.

### Memory over sessions

Let people return without losing context. The current Profile and longitudinal
Timeline carry progress across visits; session boundaries must not make care feel
fragmented or force patients to reconstruct what HealthOS already knows.

### Configuration over code

Approved changeable policy is versioned configuration with owners and effective
dates. Safety, authority, audit, and forbidden transitions remain hard boundaries.

### Capabilities over features

Design reusable abilities that serve many products and care domains. Patient
language may be specific while the underlying capability stays coherent.

### Progress over purchases

Celebrate completed care steps, understanding, adherence, and improved outcomes.
Do not imply that buying medication is equivalent to receiving or improving care.

### Evidence over assumptions

Show what is known, unknown, reported, measured, corrected, or awaiting review.
Never manufacture precision, confidence, urgency, or eligibility.

### Human judgment over automation

Automation reduces burden and highlights evidence. Accountable people retain
judgment where policy, clinical authority, or meaningful discretion requires it.

### AI assists; Providers decide

Generated assistance is labeled, evidence-linked, reviewable, and correctable.
Provider approval or override is separately attributable; Care Team access alone
cannot prescribe.

### Natural language over medical jargon

Prefer plain, respectful language and define necessary clinical terms. Internal
canonical terms stay precise; patient presentation adapts without changing meaning.

### Transparency over false certainty

Explain why a step is required, what happens next, and when information is missing.
Distinguish ordered, under review, approved, purchased, filled, shipped, and delivered.

### Agency with safety

Give people meaningful control over preferences, consent, communication, and
participation while clearly identifying decisions reserved for licensed authority.

### Accessible by default

Information hierarchy, language, interaction, motion, color, and media support
different abilities, devices, health literacy, and emotional states.

### Trust and privacy over spectacle

Use warm-white space, restrained color, quiet motion, clear hierarchy, and low-noise
surfaces to create an Apple-like sense of calm and consideration. “Garden of Eden”
is an emotional direction—safe, optimistic, restorative—not a literal decorative
theme. Privacy cues and control should feel integral, not hidden in legal chrome.

### Clinical clarity over aesthetic minimalism

Minimalism never hides urgent symptoms, safety holds, contraindications, deadlines,
costs, consent, or uncertainty. Urgent clinical information remains appropriately
prominent and accessible even when it interrupts the calm visual rhythm.

## Experience tests

An experience is Canon-aligned when a patient can answer:

1. Where am I in my health journey?
2. What is the next meaningful step and why?
3. What is known, unknown, or waiting for a person?
4. Who made or will make the decision?
5. What will happen to my treatment, payment, order, or shipment next?
6. How can I ask for help, correct information, or contest an outcome?

## Anti-patterns

Urgency theater; dense dashboards by default; celebratory checkout as a clinical
outcome; hidden generated content; medical jargon as authority signaling; silent
eligibility failure; raw pharmacy SKUs in patient browsing; dark patterns around
membership, consent, cancellation, or pricing.

## Acceptance criteria

- Critical clinical, financial, and privacy meaning remains explicit.
- Patient language maps to stable canonical objects and states.
- Generated assistance and human decisions are visually and semantically distinct.
- Accessibility and reduced cognitive load are review gates, not polish tasks.
- The Timeline communicates progress without flattening uncertainty or history.
